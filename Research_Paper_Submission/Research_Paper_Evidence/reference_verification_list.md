# Reference Verification List

All academic sources cited in the research paper have been rigorously cross-checked against scientific databases (Crossref, PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, and Elsevier ScienceDirect). **No synthetic, hallucinated, or unverified references are permitted.**

---

### [1] Breiman (2001)
* **Authors:** Leo Breiman
* **Title:** Random Forests
* **Journal:** *Machine Learning*
* **Volume / Issue / Pages:** Vol. 45, No. 1, pp. 5–32
* **Year:** 2001
* **DOI:** [10.1023/A:1010933404324](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1010933404324)
* **Relevance:** Foundational reference for the Random Forest ensemble algorithm, bagging, out-of-bag error estimation, and random feature subspace splitting.

---

### [2] Breiman et al. (1984)
* **Authors:** Leo Breiman, Jerome H. Friedman, Richard A. Olshen, and Charles J. Stone
* **Title:** Classification and Regression Trees
* **Publisher:** Wadsworth & Brooks/Cole Advanced Books & Software, Monterey, CA
* **Year:** 1984
* **ISBN:** 978-0412048418
* **Relevance:** Foundational formulation of binary recursive partitioning, impurity reduction, and CART regression trees.

---

### [3] van Klompenburg, Kassahun, & Catal (2020)
* **Authors:** Thomas van Klompenburg, Ayalew Kassahun, and Cemal Catal
* **Title:** Crop yield prediction using machine learning: A systematic literature review
* **Journal:** *Computers and Electronics in Agriculture*
* **Volume / Article ID:** Vol. 177, Article 105709
* **Year:** 2020
* **DOI:** [10.1016/j.compag.2020.105709](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2020.105709)
* **Relevance:** Systematic review identifying temperature, rainfall, and soil type as the primary predictive agricultural features, and surveying ML model families.

---

### [4] Chlingaryan, Sukkarieh, & Whelan (2018)
* **Authors:** Anna Chlingaryan, Salah Sukkarieh, and Brett Whelan
* **Title:** Machine learning approaches for crop yield prediction and nitrogen status estimation in precision agriculture: A review
* **Journal:** *Computers and Electronics in Agriculture*
* **Volume / Pages:** Vol. 151, pp. 61–69
* **Year:** 2018
* **DOI:** [10.1016/j.compag.2018.05.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2018.05.012)
* **Relevance:** Comprehensive survey on empirical machine learning approaches, non-linear biological relationships, and multi-source input features in precision farming.

---

### [5] Jeong et al. (2016)
* **Authors:** Jig Han Jeong, Jonathan P. Resop, Nathaniel D. Mueller, David H. Fleisher, Kyungdahm Yun, Ethan E. Butler, Dennis J. Timlin, Kyo-Moon Shim, James S. Gerber, Vangimalla R. Reddy, and Soo-Hyung Kim
* **Title:** Random forests for global and regional crop yield predictions
* **Journal:** *PLoS ONE*
* **Volume / Issue / Article:** Vol. 11, No. 6, Article e0156571
* **Year:** 2016
* **DOI:** [10.1371/journal.pone.0156571](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0156571)
* **Relevance:** Benchmark study establishing Random Forests outperforming Multiple Linear Regression in agricultural yield estimation, while highlighting tree model extrapolation limits at distribution boundaries.

---

### [6] Chicco, Warrens, & Jurman (2021)
* **Authors:** Davide Chicco, Matthijs J. Warrens, and Giuseppe Jurman
* **Title:** The coefficient of determination R-squared is more informative than SMAPE, MAE, MAPE, MSE and RMSE in regression analysis evaluation
* **Journal:** *PeerJ Computer Science*
* **Volume / Article:** Vol. 7, Article e623
* **Year:** 2021
* **DOI:** [10.7717/peerj-cs.623](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj-cs.623)
* **Relevance:** Theoretical and empirical justification for the Coefficient of Determination ($R^2$) as a normalized, scale-free metric of variance explanation relative to the ground truth mean.

---

### [7] Willmott & Matsuura (2005)
* **Authors:** Cort J. Willmott and Kenji Matsuura
* **Title:** Advantages of the mean absolute error (MAE) over the root mean squared error (RMSE) in assessing average model performance
* **Journal:** *Climate Research*
* **Volume / Issue / Pages:** Vol. 30, No. 1, pp. 79–82
* **Year:** 2005
* **DOI:** [10.3354/cr030079](https://doi.org/10.3354/cr030079)
* **Relevance:** Essential methodological paper establishing MAE as an unambiguous measure of average error magnitude uninflated by error variance or sample size.

---

### [8] Chai & Draxler (2014)
* **Authors:** Tianfeng Chai and Roland R. Draxler
* **Title:** Root mean square error (RMSE) or mean absolute error (MAE)? – Arguments against avoiding RMSE in the literature
* **Journal:** *Geoscientific Model Development*
* **Volume / Issue / Pages:** Vol. 7, No. 3, pp. 1247–1250
* **Year:** 2014
* **DOI:** [10.5194/gmd-7-1247-2014](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-7-1247-2014)
* **Relevance:** Complementary metric justification showing why reporting both MAE and RMSE reveals error distribution kurtosis and sensitivity to extreme residual deviations.

### [13] Roberts et al. (2017)
* **Authors:** David R. Roberts, Volker Bahn, Simone Ciuti, Mark S. Boyce, Jane Elith, Gurutzeta Guillera-Arroita, Severin Hauenstein, José J. Lahoz-Monfort, Boris Schröder, Wilfried Thuiller, and David I. Warton

* **Journal:** *Ecography*
* **Volume / Issue / Pages:** Vol. 40, No. 8, pp. 913–929
* **Year:** 2017
* **DOI:** [10.1111/ecog.02881](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881)
